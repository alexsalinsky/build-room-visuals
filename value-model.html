Four ways a robot pays

01

Hours back

The obvious one. Work that took six hours takes twenty minutes, every time, from now on.

02

Same build, more desks

Built once, adopted by the whole team at no extra cost. This is where the real money is and almost nobody measures it.

03

The quote you never pay

What an agency or developer would have charged for the same thing. Owners remember these numbers exactly.

04

The raised ceiling

Some builders don't save hours, they serve four times the clients. That's not a cost line, it's a revenue line.

The part that gets missed

The build takes one month. The savings come back every month after that. So you're comparing something you pay for once against something that keeps paying, and the gap widens every month while the cost stays exactly where it was.

Which is why the tool isn't really the point. A cohort that produces one robot is worth what it costs. A cohort that produces a person who can build the next one, and the one after that, is worth something else entirely.
