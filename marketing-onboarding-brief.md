## 1. What The Build Room is

**The three rules (the most important thing in this document):**

> **Learn AI. Build Robots. Stay Human.**

That line is the brand. It appears verbatim, never paraphrased, in every asset that uses it.

The Build Room takes regular people (operators, founders, and their teams) and has them build something real with AI in four weeks. Cohort-based, outcome-oriented: everyone ships by Demo Day. Founder-led brand. Don't position it as "AI training" or "AI fluency"; the product is the transformation, the confidence of having built something yourself.

**The purpose behind it** (from Alex's Horizons of Focus, drafted 2026-07-12): watch people transform. Alex uses everything he's built (tech since 6th grade, 10 years in Silicon Valley, a teacher's heart, real facilitation skill) to take regular people to the next level, and get paid well doing it. The win is their confidence: crossing from hopeless to empowered and still human. AI is the vehicle, not the point.

**Non-negotiable principles:**
1. Technology serves us, not drives us.
2. You will change. You will make a product. This is not a course.
3. Nobody rejects themselves on my watch.
4. No step-by-step framework. You tackle your problem, with coaching.
5. No fabricated proof. Real wins, real names (with permission), real numbers.
6. The emotional element is permanent.

**The format:** 4 weeks, 1 live session per week (~2 hours each), builds between sessions, Demo Day at the end. Sessions run on Zoom with breakout coaching. A "Session Zero" kickoff precedes S1-S4.

## 6. The obvious gaps (be blunt with us)

1. **No owner for marketing.** Alex's own words in the Horizons doc: "I don't know how to do it. Needs an owner or a system." This is the seat being recruited for.
2. **No attribution.** Nobody knows which channel produces buyers. No UTMs enforced, no "where did you hear about us" capture at checkout.
3. **Proof is trapped.** 155 of 178 proof entries are raw. The best marketing material the business owns is sitting unpublished for lack of permission asks and clip cutting.
4. **Content cadence is sporadic.** 3 newsletter editions in 5 months, no talks or podcasts shipped, finished reels sitting unposted. Assets get built and then stall at the publish step.
5. **The closed-cohort (corporate) pipeline doesn't exist.** It's the existential goal (first business-team client by Mar 2027) and today it's two warm names and one conference.
6. **CAC is unknown.** No paid channel has ever been tested. A $200 LinkedIn ad experiment is planned for PDC, gated on venue lock.
7. **Small audience.** A recent 30-day engagement scrape found ~16 LinkedIn reactors and ~12 IG engagers. Top of funnel is thin; the launch engine assumes audiences that don't exist yet.
8. **Founder bottleneck.** Alex travels Sep-Nov (London, cruise, Mexico, Bangkok, Chiang Mai) exactly when all three launches peak. Anything designed must run without him some weeks.
9. **Alumni layer undefined.** Membership format + price is parked until Feb 2027, so post-cohort energy has nowhere to go except Skool.

## 8. Brand and voice rules (hard, already decided, don't re-litigate)

- Thesis line verbatim wherever used: "Learn AI. Build Robots. Stay Human."
- The live event is called a "Kickoff Session." Never "webinar" or "masterclass."
- No em dashes in any copy. Ever.
- Stripe link in chat ONCE per Kickoff Session. No pressure closes.
- No fabricated proof, quotes, or testimonials. Only Proof Library entries marked cleared-for-public or better go public. Real names only with permission.
- No invented URLs or domains (buildroom.work does not exist).
- No black-box visual treatment in decks. No "drop it in the chat" audience prompts.
- Cohort ads: no. Retreat ads: small test only, kill if CAC > 2x tier-1 margin.
- Skool community stays engagement-gated, not open-by-affiliate-link.
- Voice: direct, peer-to-peer, contractions on, no corporate filler. Samples: the LinkedIn newsletter and jess-pack/01-tone-of-voice.md.
- Never publish quotes where a builder puts someone down, even cleared ones.
- Sliding-scale and per-builder performance details stay private. Client-facing artifacts show role + name only.
